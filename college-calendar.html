What a college calendar has to do that a work calendar does not

An office calendar is built around meetings other people send you. A semester is built around a fixed timetable plus a long tail of deadlines, most of them decided in week one and written down in five different documents. The calendar that works for a student is the one that starts full: every class meeting, every due date, every exam, before you have added a single thing yourself.

That is how Semester HQ’s calendar starts. Add a class with its meeting days and times and every session appears through the end of the term. Upload the syllabus and the deadlines and exam dates join them. From there it behaves the way you would hope: four views, one color per class, and dragging anything to a new day moves it. Study sessions, project milestones, and your club’s practices land on the same grid, because they take the same hours.

How to build your college calendar in five steps

The first two steps fill the calendar. The last three are how you keep using it in week nine.

  1. Add each class once

    In Courses, add a class with its meeting days, times, and room. Every meeting appears on the calendar for the rest of the term. Pick a color and it follows the class everywhere.

  2. Let the syllabus fill in the dates

    With Semester HQ Plus, upload each syllabus as a PDF, Word, PowerPoint, or Excel file, a photo, or pasted text. Assignments and exam dates land on the calendar in about a minute, and the office hours and attendance rule land on the class page. Check the dates, fix anything it misread, and move on.

  3. Choose the view for the job

    Month for planning around a heavy week. Week for the shape of the next seven days. Day for the morning of. Year for the whole semester at once, which is the view to open before you agree to anything in November.

  4. Plan work, not just deadlines

    Drag an assignment onto a day to reschedule it, or onto a time slot to decide when you will actually do it. Use Time block for a study session with a start and an end. Sessions you plan from an exam’s prep page land here too.

  5. Bring in the rest of the term

    Join a study group and its sessions appear with RSVPs. Join a club and every practice, meeting, and dues deadline an officer posts shows up next to your classes. Project milestones and application deadlines do the same. One grid, every color, nothing copied across by hand.

Questions about the college calendar

Does it show my recurring classes automatically?

Yes. Add a class with its meeting days and times once, and every session appears on the calendar for the rest of the semester. Upload the syllabus and the class times come from there instead.

Can I see the whole semester, not just one month?

Yes. There is a year view alongside month, week, and day. It is the fastest way to see where the exams cluster and which weeks are quiet.

Does the calendar sync with Google Calendar?

No. Semester HQ keeps its own calendar and does not push events into Google Calendar or Apple Calendar. Everything it holds is in one place, on the web and on your phone. If you live in Google Calendar and your professors publish feeds, that may honestly be enough for you.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. It runs in the phone’s browser with no app store download, and you can add it to your home screen so it opens like an app and works offline. The month, week, day, and year views work the same on a laptop, a phone, or a library computer.

Will it send me reminders?

No. Semester HQ sends no notifications of any kind: no push, no permission prompt, nothing on a lock screen. The Heads Up bell in the corner gathers what is overdue, due today and tomorrow, and coming up, for whenever you open it.
