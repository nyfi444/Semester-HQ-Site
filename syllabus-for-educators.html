For educators

Write a syllabus your students can plan from.

Many students keep their semester in Semester HQ. They upload your syllabus and the class times, every deadline, the exam dates, your contact details, office hours, the attendance limit, and the late policy fill in. A few formatting habits make that work on the first try, and they make the syllabus easier for every student to read.

What your students’ planner reads from a syllabus

Students upload your syllabus as a PDF, a Word, PowerPoint, or Excel file, a photo of a printed copy, or pasted text. Semester HQ reads it and fills in their class:

  • The course name, code, instructor, and room, plus the meeting days and times.
  • Every assignment, reading, quiz, exam, project, paper, and lab, with its due date and time.
  • Your email, phone, and office, your office hours with days, times, and place, and each TA’s name, email, and hours.
  • The number of absences allowed, the attendance and late work policies in a sentence or two, and a few other rules students ask about, such as makeup work and missed exams.
  • The course website and the textbook.

Students check what it found before anything is saved, and they can edit all of it. There is no instructor account and nothing for you to install. Everything on this page is about the document you already write.

How to format a syllabus that reads cleanly in seven steps

None of these are special to Semester HQ. They are the same habits that help a student skimming the PDF at midnight, and they help the upload get every date right the first time.

  1. Keep the course code in the header

    Put the code, title, term, and section at the top: “BIO 201: Cell Biology, Fall 2026, Section 03.” The code is how students tell their classes apart, and it labels the class in their planner.

  2. Write full dates with the year

    Write “Tuesday, September 29, 2026” or “9/29/2026,” not “Week 5, Tuesday.” A week number cannot go on a calendar, and a date without a year leaves room to guess.

  3. Put one assignment on each line of a schedule table

    Use a table with a date, what is due, and a time, one row per item. “Quiz 3 and Lab 4 report” in one cell is easy to misread as one deadline. Two rows are not.

  4. State every exam date and time

    Write “Midterm 1: Thursday, October 15, 2026, 9:30 to 10:45 am, in class.” If the final is set by the registrar, say so, and say when the date will be posted.

  5. Give office hours days, times, and a place

    Write “Office hours: Tuesdays and Thursdays, 2:00 to 3:30 pm, Science Hall 214, or by appointment.” Each day and time becomes a block students can put on their calendar. List TA hours the same way, with each TA’s name and email.

  6. Put a number on the attendance rule

    “3 absences allowed” is something a student can count. Semester HQ lets each student keep a private tally of their absences against that number, so they know where they stand. “Attendance is important” gives them nothing to track.

  7. State the late policy in one or two sentences

    Write “Late work loses 10% per day for up to 3 days, then it is not accepted.” Add the makeup and missed exam rules the same way. The upload keeps a one or two sentence summary of each policy, so a short, specific rule comes through intact.

What students see after the upload

The syllabus panel on a class page, filled in from one uploaded file.

If your course runs on Canvas, Blackboard, Brightspace, or Moodle

Students can also paste their calendar feed link from Canvas, Blackboard, Brightspace, or Moodle into Semester HQ. Every dated item comes across, sorted into the right class. When you move a deadline in the LMS, it moves in their planner too, the next time they open it.

The feed and the syllabus do different jobs. The feed keeps due dates current. The syllabus carries what the LMS never has: meeting times, office hours, the attendance rule, and the late policy. One tip: a feed refresh never deletes anything, so if you cancel an assignment, say so in class or in an announcement.

Questions from educators

Do I need an account for my students to use this?

No. There is no instructor account. Students upload the syllabus you already share. The formatting tips on this page only make that upload more complete.

Which file types can students upload?

A PDF, a Word, PowerPoint, or Excel file, a photo of a printed page, or pasted text. Students check and edit what it found before anything is saved.

What happens when I change a due date mid-semester?

If students connected your course’s Canvas, Blackboard, Brightspace, or Moodle calendar feed, the new date comes across the next time they open Semester HQ. If they only uploaded the syllabus, announce the change as you normally would and they update the date themselves.

Can I buy Semester HQ for my whole section?

Yes. Group plans are $5.99 per member per month, self-serve for 5 to 50 seats at app.semester-hq.com/group-admin.html. For more than 50 seats, or for an invoice, a PO, or a W-9, use the quote form on semester-hq.com/group-pricing.html.

Does Semester HQ send my students reminders?

No. Semester HQ sends no notifications. Your deadlines sit on each student’s calendar, in their assignments list, and in the Heads up panel whenever they open the app.
