What actually happens when you upload one

Hand over the syllabus as a PDF, a Word, PowerPoint, or Excel file, a photo of a printed copy, or pasted text. Semester HQ reads it and pulls out the meeting days and times, the assignment schedule, every exam date it can find, the professor’s contact details, office hours, the TA, and the attendance and late-work policies. The dates go on your calendar and into the assignments list. The details go on the class’s own page, with a private tally of your absences against the limit.

Nothing is locked in. Everything it read sits in front of you to check and edit, and the original file stays in the class page so you can open it again in week twelve. To do the reading, the file is sent to Anthropic’s Claude API; Anthropic does not use it to train models and does not keep it beyond its API data retention policy.

How to turn a syllabus into a plan in five steps

About a minute per class once you have the file. The checking step is the one worth slowing down for.

  1. Get the file, in whatever form it exists

    Download the PDF from your LMS, photograph the printed copy, or copy the text out of an email. Word, PowerPoint, and Excel files work too, which matters for the professor who keeps the schedule in a spreadsheet.

  2. Upload it from Courses

    Open Courses, choose Upload syllabus, and pick the file or paste the text. Syllabus upload is part of Semester HQ Plus; the demo shows the interface but cannot read a file.

  3. Check what it found

    In about a minute you see the class times, every assignment and exam date, the professor’s email, office hours, the TA, the attendance limit, and the late policy, laid out for review. Fix a misread date, rename an assignment, delete a row that was only a suggested reading. Then save.

  4. Open the class page

    The dates are now on your calendar and in Assignments, color-coded. The class page holds the rest: a one-tap email to the professor, the next office hours highlighted, and an absence tracker reading 1 of 3 allowed absences used.

  5. Share it with your section

    One person uploads. Classmates join with a code, a link, or by searching your school, and get every date in one tap. When a deadline moves, everyone’s copy follows. Only the schedule is shared, never your progress, notes, or absences.

Questions about the syllabus planner

Does it work with any syllabus format?

Yes. Upload a PDF, Word, PowerPoint, or Excel file, take a photo of a printed syllabus, or paste the text. It reads whichever you give it and pulls out the same details either way. A syllabus with no dates in it, only “see Canvas”, will give you the class details and policies, and you add the dates by typing them.

What if it gets something wrong?

Everything it adds is shown for review before it is final, and everything stays editable afterwards. Fix a wrong date or add something it missed in a few seconds, the same as anything you typed yourself.

Is my syllabus stored anywhere after I upload it?

Yes, in your class page, so you can open it again later. It stays until you delete the file or your account. To extract the details, the file is sent to Anthropic’s Claude API. Anthropic does not use it to train models and does not keep it beyond its API data retention policy. Semester HQ never sells your data.

Can I upload a syllabus in the demo?

No. The demo runs the full interface with a sample semester and saves nothing, but reading a file needs an account. Syllabus upload is part of Semester HQ Plus at $7.99 a month, with a full refund on the first charge if you email within 14 days.

Does it read the attendance policy too?

Yes. The attendance limit and the late-work policy land on the class page, and a private absence tracker counts against that limit as you mark days missed. Classmates never see it, even in a shared class.
