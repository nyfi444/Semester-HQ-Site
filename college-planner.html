What a college planner should actually do

Most planner apps were built for offices and relabeled for students. They do not know that classes exist, that a semester has a shape, or that the most important document you own is a PDF a professor handed you in week one. So the setup is an evening of retyping, and the planner is abandoned by week three.

Semester HQ is built around the semester itself. Each class has a page holding what is due next, the next exam with a countdown, the office hours, the attendance rule, your notes, and your flashcards. Above the classes sit one calendar and one Assignments list. Around them: exam prep, projects, study groups, clubs, and an applications tracker. It does not track grades. It tracks the work, the dates, and the people.

How to set up a college planner in five steps

One sitting, ideally in the first week, with every syllabus you have been given so far.

  1. Start with semester setup

    Sign in with Google or any email. Semester setup asks for your term dates and your classes. Add each class with its meeting days and times, or skip ahead to the syllabus.

  2. Upload every syllabus, then read each class page once

    With Semester HQ Plus, hand over each syllabus as a PDF, Word, PowerPoint, or Excel file, a photo of a printed copy, or pasted text. In about a minute per class you get class times, every assignment and exam date, the professor’s contact details, office hours, the TA, the attendance limit, and the late policy, all editable. Open each class page once to confirm the dates and read the policies while they are fresh. From now on that page answers the questions you used to reopen the PDF for.

  3. Add the rest by typing it like a text

    “psych quiz 2 thursday 11am” becomes a quiz for PSY 101 due Thursday at 11:00 AM. Quick add picks out the class, day, time, and type while you type. Repeating work becomes a series. A paper becomes a project with milestones.

  4. Bring in your people

    Share a class so your section gets every deadline in one tap; only the schedule is shared, never your progress or notes. Start a study group for a hard class, find the hour everyone is free, and split the work. Join your club or team and its events land on your calendar too.

  5. Run the week from the calendar and the dashboard

    Everything sits on one calendar in month, week, day, or year view, color-coded by class. Drag assignments onto the day you will do them, and add time blocks for study sessions. Each morning the dashboard shows which week of the term you are in, what is up next, what is due this week, and the next exams. Nothing notifies you; you come to it.

Questions about Semester HQ as a college planner

How is this different from Canvas or my school’s LMS?

Canvas is run by your school and holds only what each professor puts in it. Semester HQ is yours: it also holds your own notes, to-dos, study sessions, study groups, club events, and anything from a class that never touched the LMS. It also carries across every semester. They are not an either/or: paste your Canvas calendar link into Semester HQ and the dates your professors do publish come across on their own, so the only things you type are the ones Canvas never had.

Do I need a school email to use it?

No. Sign in with any Google account or any email address. No school affiliation, campus login, or IT approval is needed, and it works the same at a four-year college, a community college, or in grad school.

Is there a free plan?

No. There is a live demo of the full interface that needs no account and saves nothing, so you can see everything before you pay. Semester HQ Plus is $7.99 a month, cancel anytime, with a full refund on the first charge if you email within 14 days.

Does it track grades or GPA?

No, by design. Semester HQ tracks what is due, when classes meet, when exams are, and how prepared you are. Grades live wherever your school keeps them.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. It runs in the phone’s browser, installs to the home screen, and works offline. Your semester is the same on a laptop, a phone, or a library computer once you sign in.
