Student planner

A student planner built around your semester, not a template.

Studio critiques, problem sets, lab reports, reading-heavy seminars, clinical hours. Every major runs differently, and a planner built for one shape of coursework fights you the moment your classes do not match it. Semester HQ is organized by class and by due date, and bends to the rest.

Built to bend to your major, not the other way around

A student planner has one job: make the next thing obvious. That gets harder the less your week looks like a timetable. An engineering student has weekly problem sets and a lab every other Tuesday. A nursing student has lectures, clinicals, and a skills check-off. An art student has a critique and a studio open until midnight. The same app has to work for all three without asking anyone to fill in a field that makes no sense.

So Semester HQ keeps the fixed parts small. Every class gets a color that follows it through the calendar, the assignments list, and the notebook. Work carries a type, so the list tells you what kind of hour you are in for. And nothing assumes a shape to your term: add a class mid-semester, archive one you dropped, or plan a thesis that spans two.

How to set up a student planner in five steps

An afternoon, and only the second step needs any thought.

  1. Sign in and name the term

    Any Google account or email works. Semester setup asks for the term’s start and end dates, so the dashboard can tell you which week you are in.

  2. Add your classes with their real rhythm

    Give each class its meeting days, times, and room. A lab that meets every other week, a seminar that meets once, a studio with open hours: enter what is true, not what a template expects. Pick a color and a name you will recognize, because both follow the class everywhere. “Orgo” works better than a course code you never say out loud. With Plus, upload the syllabus instead and the times, dates, and policies come from the document.

  3. Put in the work, typed like a text

    “lab report 3 monday 5pm” becomes a lab for the right class due Monday at 5:00 PM. Readings and problem sets that repeat become a series. A capstone becomes a project with milestones.

  4. Keep notes and cards where the class is

    The notebook files every note under its class, with highlights, checklists, export to PDF, and search. Turn a note into flashcards, or import a Quizlet, Anki, or CSV deck, and spaced repetition brings each card back right before you would forget it.

  5. Make the dashboard match your week

    The dashboard shows what is up next, today’s schedule, what is due this week, the next exams, and a weekly focus goal. Turn off the parts you do not use. Nothing interrupts you; the Heads Up bell gathers what needs attention. When your program changes, so does the planner: add, edit, and archive classes and semesters at any point.

Questions about the student planner

Is this only for four-year college students?

No. Anyone taking classes with due dates and a schedule can use it: four-year college, community college, grad school, nursing or trade programs. No university affiliation is required.

Does it work for grad school?

Yes. Classes, seminars, lab hours, and a thesis with milestones all fit, and nothing assumes an undergraduate timetable. A project that spans two terms works too.

What if I switch majors or add a minor?

Nothing in the planner assumes a fixed major. Add, edit, or archive classes and semesters at any point as your program changes.

Does it grade my work or calculate a GPA?

No. Semester HQ deliberately leaves grades out. It tracks what is due, when classes meet, exams, and how prepared you feel.
