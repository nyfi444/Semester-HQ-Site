A schedule is more than the blocks on a grid

The colorful timetable you make in week one answers one question: where am I at 10:00 on Tuesday. It goes quiet the moment you need anything else. When are Dr. Patel’s office hours. How many absences are allowed in Calculus II before it costs you. Is Midterm 1 the week before or the week after fall break.

Semester HQ builds the grid and keeps the rest. Add a class, or upload its syllabus, and every meeting appears in the week view for the whole term. The class page holds the office hours with the next one highlighted, a one-tap email to the professor, the TA, the late policy, and an absence tracker that counts against the limit in the syllabus. Exam dates get a countdown and a prep page. It is one schedule that knows what the blocks are for.

How to make your class schedule in five steps

Do this once in week one. The last three steps are the ones a paper timetable cannot do.

  1. Add each class, or upload its syllabus

    In Courses, add a class with its meeting days, times, and room, and every session appears in the week view through the end of the term. With Semester HQ Plus, upload the syllabus instead, as a PDF, Word, PowerPoint, or Excel file, a photo, or pasted text, and the times come from the document along with everything in the next three steps.

  2. Check the week view

    Open Calendar and switch to Week. Each class has its color. Look for the collisions you would not spot on paper: a lab that runs into a seminar, a Friday with nothing until 3:00 PM, the Monday that starts at 8:00 AM. Fix a misread time on the class page and it updates everywhere.

  3. Put office hours where you can find them

    Each class page lists the professor’s office hours with the next one highlighted, and the TA’s hours below, with a one-tap Email button next to the name. This is the part of a schedule most students lose by week three.

  4. Know the attendance rule before you need it

    The syllabus says how many absences are allowed. Semester HQ reads it and keeps a private tracker on the class page: 1 of 3 allowed absences used. Mark a day missed and the count moves. Classmates never see it, even when the class is shared.

  5. Mark the exam dates and plan around them

    Every exam from the syllabus gets a live countdown and its own prep page. List the topics, rate each one not yet, shaky, or got it, and place study sessions on the calendar in the weeks before. Open the year view once to see where the exams cluster.

Questions about the class schedule maker

Can it make my schedule from the syllabus alone?

Mostly, yes. Upload the syllabus and the meeting days and times, exam dates, office hours, and attendance rule are read out and laid onto the week view and the class page in about a minute, ready to check. If a syllabus does not list the times, add them by hand once and everything else still comes from the document.

Does it show office hours on the schedule?

Office hours live on each class page, with the next one highlighted and a one-tap email to the professor. They are not added to the calendar grid automatically, so the week view stays your class meetings and your own plans.

How does the absence tracker work?

The attendance limit is read from the syllabus, or you type it in. Mark a day missed on the class page and the tracker shows where you stand, for example 1 of 3 allowed absences used. It is private, even in a shared class.

Can I export the schedule to Google Calendar?

No. Semester HQ keeps its own calendar, on the web and installed on your phone, and does not export to Google Calendar or Apple Calendar. If a feed is what you need, Google Calendar with repeating events is the honest answer.

What if I add or drop a class mid-semester?

Add the new class and its meetings appear on the week view at once. Archive the one you dropped and its meetings and deadlines leave the calendar.
